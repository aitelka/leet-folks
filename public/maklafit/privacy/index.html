Privacy Policy for Makla Fit

Effective 4 September 2026 · Last updated 4 September 2026

This policy explains what the Makla Fit Android application (package com.leetfolks.maklafit, the “App”) does with information when you use it. The App is published by LeetFolks (“we”, “us”).

The short version. Makla Fit is a food and fitness diary. There is no account and no sign-up.

What you eat, drink, weigh and lift is stored only on your device. We operate no server that receives your diary, and it is excluded from Android cloud backup.

Two things do leave your device. A food you create is published to a shared food database so the next person who scans that barcode finds it — with no identifier saying it came from you. And anonymous usage statistics, crash reports and advertising data go to Google.

None of what you log — no meal, weight, or workout — is ever part of that.

1. Your diary, which stays on your device

To be useful the App needs to know things about you: a name you choose for your profile, your age, gender, height, current weight, activity level and goal, and then what you log each day — meals and portions, water, weight readings, and workouts with their sets, reps and durations.

All of this is written to a private database on your device and is never transmitted to us. We operate no server that receives it and hold no copy of it. Specifically:

  • Your profile, food log, water log, weight history, workout history and any recipes you build are held in a database inside the App’s own storage area, which other apps cannot read.
  • That database is excluded from Android cloud backup, so your health history is never uploaded to Google Drive.
  • It is included in a direct device-to-device transfer — the user-initiated move between two phones you are holding yourself — because that is the case where losing your history would actually hurt. It goes straight from the old phone to the new one.

2. Foods you create, which are shared publicly

The App’s value depends on a food being findable by barcode. When you create a food — typically after scanning a barcode the App does not yet recognise — that nutrition record is uploaded to a shared database (Google Cloud Firestore) so the next person who scans the same barcode finds it already filled in.

What is uploaded is the food record and nothing else:

  • The name you gave it, including its Arabic name, and its category;
  • Serving size and unit;
  • Nutrition per 100g: calories, protein, carbohydrate, fat, fibre, sugar, saturated fat, trans fat, cholesterol, sodium, potassium, vitamin A, vitamin C, calcium and iron;
  • The barcode, a product image URL where one exists, and the time the record was created.

No identifier of any kind travels with it. The record does not carry your name, an account, a device identifier, or anything linking it back to you, because the App has no such identifier to attach. Nothing from your diary is uploaded: not the fact that you ate the food, not when, not how much.

These records are public. Any copy of the App can read the shared food database — that is the point of it. Please do not type personal information into a food name or an Arabic name, because unlike your diary, that field is not private.

The App cannot delete a shared record once written. If you need one corrected or removed, write to the address at the bottom of this page with the barcode and we will handle it.

3. The camera

The App asks for camera permission for one purpose: reading a barcode on a package, or a QR code on a piece of gym equipment. You are asked only at the moment you open the scanner, and the App works without granting it — you can search and enter foods by hand instead.

Scanning happens entirely on your device, using Google’s ML Kit running locally. Camera frames are examined in memory to find a code and are then discarded. No photograph is saved, and no image is ever uploaded to us or to anyone else.

Gym equipment QR codes from Life Fitness machines are decoded on your device as well: the App reads the workout summary out of the code itself and does not contact halo.fitness or any other equipment service.

4. Information collected automatically

The App includes services from Google that send data in the background. We see these only as aggregated reports; they are not linked to your name or to any account, because there is none.

These services report how the App is used — that it was opened, that a session lasted so long, that a build crashed — and they are not given the contents of anything you log. No meal, weight reading, water entry or workout is sent to them, and none of it is used to target advertising.

Google processes the data above as described in the Google Privacy Policy. Further detail is available for Firebase and for AdMob.

5. Notifications

The App can send notifications — reminders, and occasional news about the App itself. On Android 13 and later you are asked for permission the first time the App opens, and you can refuse, or change your mind later under Android Settings → Apps → Makla Fit → Notifications. Nothing else in the App depends on it: the diary, the scanner and the catalogues all work exactly the same if you say no.

To deliver a notification, Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging issues your installation a token. It identifies the installation, not you: it carries no name, no account and nothing from your diary, and it changes if you reinstall the App or clear its data. We do not upload that token anywhere or keep a list of them, because there is no server of ours to keep it on — notifications are sent to broad audiences rather than to individuals.

6. Services your device contacts

Besides Google, the App reaches two kinds of destination. In both cases your device connects directly and we see nothing of the exchange.

  • Open Food Facts. When you scan a barcode the App does not already know, it looks the product up at world.openfoodfacts.org and may load a product photo from their image servers. That service sees your IP address and the barcode you asked about. Their handling of it is governed by their own privacy policy, not by this one.
  • Our catalogue files. The ingredient, exercise and packaged-product catalogues are downloaded from Google Firebase Storage and Cloud Firestore. These are plain file and record downloads. Nothing about you is sent as part of the request, and no record is kept of which device asked.

All of the App’s network traffic uses HTTPS. It does not permit unencrypted connections.

7. Advertising and your choices

The App shows advertising supplied by Google AdMob. Where required — including in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland — the App presents a consent form from Google’s User Messaging Platform before any personalised advertising is served, and records your answer. You may change that answer at any time, and you may withdraw consent you previously gave.

Independently of the App, Android lets you limit ad tracking or delete your advertising identifier under Settings → Privacy → Ads. Doing so does not remove advertising, but it stops it being personalised.

Your health data is never used to target advertising. Nothing you log — your weight, your meals, your workouts — is shared with AdMob or any advertiser, because it never leaves your device in the first place.

8. Health information

What you record in Makla Fit — body measurements, what you have eaten, what you have lifted — is health information, and it is treated as the most sensitive thing the App holds. It is kept on your device, excluded from cloud backup, never sold, never shared with advertisers, and never used for any purpose other than showing you your own figures inside the App.

The App is not a medical device. Its calorie and nutrition figures come from public catalogues and from records other people have entered, so they are estimates and may be wrong. Nothing in the App is medical advice, and it should not be used to diagnose or treat any condition. Talk to a qualified professional before making significant changes to your diet or training, especially if you are pregnant, managing a medical condition, or recovering from an eating disorder.

9. Retention and deletion

Everything held on your device stays there until you remove it. You can delete individual entries from within the App, clear all of it at once through Android Settings → Apps → Makla Fit → Storage → Clear data, or remove it entirely by uninstalling the App. Because we hold no copy, uninstalling is genuinely the end of it.

Food records already published to the shared database are the exception: they stay after you uninstall, since other people’s scans now depend on them. They contain nothing that identifies you. Write to us if you want one corrected or taken down.

Crash and advertising data held by Google are retained according to Google’s own schedules. To have data associated with your installation deleted from those systems, contact us at the address below and include the date and approximate time of use, since we hold no identifier that would let us find you otherwise.

10. Children

The App is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. Calorie targets and weight tracking are not designed for children, whose nutritional needs differ and who should be guided by a paediatrician rather than by an app. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

11. Security

Your diary is stored in private application storage that other apps cannot access, and is excluded from cloud backup so it is not copied off the device. All network connections the App makes are encrypted with HTTPS.

No account exists to be broken into, and no server of ours holds your history, which removes the largest category of risk. The trade-off is that a lost or wiped phone means a lost diary unless you transfer it directly to a new device.

12. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete, or port your personal data, to object to or restrict its processing, and to withdraw consent. Residents of the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom hold these rights under the GDPR; residents of California hold comparable rights under the CCPA, including the right not to be discriminated against for exercising them. We do not sell personal information.

Because the App has no accounts, almost nothing reaches us, and what does is not linked to your identity. To exercise any of these rights, or to complain to a supervisory authority, use the contact address below.

13. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as the App changes. The effective date at the top will be revised, and material changes will be signalled in the App or on its Google Play listing. Continuing to use the App after a change means you accept the revised policy.
